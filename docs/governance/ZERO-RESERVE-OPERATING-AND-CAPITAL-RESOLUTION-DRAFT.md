# Golden Obsidian LLC — Zero-Reserve Operating and Capital Resolution (Draft)

> **STATUS: DRAFT FOR GOVERNING-PERSON REVIEW — NOT ADOPTED, NOT SIGNED, AND NOT AUTHORITY TO SPEND OR CONTRACT.**
>
> This document is a proposed business-control framework and a draft form of company action. It is not legal, tax, securities, employment, or accounting advice. It must be conformed to the Company's actual jurisdiction of formation, certificate of formation, company agreement/operating agreement, ownership, tax election, and approval requirements before anyone signs or relies on it.

**Prepared:** 2026-10-31
**Company:** Golden Obsidian LLC
**Project:** SystemFabric
**Financial starting point supplied by the owner:** no funding and no liquid asset reserve; no approved resolution, budget, payment program, or authority to commit funds to employees, contractors, assessors, vendors, suppliers, platforms, or other organizations. This statement is a planning assumption from the owner, not an independently audited financial statement.

## 1. Executive recommendation

Adopt a **zero-reserve, revenue-first operating model**. Do not try to solve a zero-cash position by informally promising future pay, recruiting unpaid staff, issuing improvised equity, using personal accounts to collect project money, taking on debt without a repayment plan, or marketing SystemFabric as production-ready.

The Company should separate three things that have previously been bundled together:

1. **Market development:** permitted now on a no-cost, non-binding basis, with accurate developer-preview disclosures.
2. **Commercial revenue:** pursue founder-led software engineering and narrowly scoped customer work that can be contracted and delivered without representing SystemFabric 0.5.1 as production-certified. Accept a paid pilot only when its scope, delivery capacity, funds, customer-data controls, and obligations are understood and documented.
3. **Capital and hiring:** no securities offering, borrowing, paid hiring, paid assessor engagement, purchase order, or recurring spend until the Company has the correct approvals, a verified funding source, and a separate written authorization.

The first funding objective is **not broad expansion**. It is to create a small, dependable operating reserve and pay for the highest-risk independent evidence work, beginning with the independent tenant-isolation assessment. The product stays a developer preview until its existing release gates are satisfied.

## 2. Proposed resolution — operative text for approval

The following is proposed wording for a written consent or resolution by the Company's actual governing authority. It is deliberately conservative. The approval process, signatories, voting threshold, and whether members or managers act must be confirmed against the Company's governing documents and jurisdiction before execution.

### Resolution of Golden Obsidian LLC

**WHEREAS**, the Company currently has no funding or liquid asset reserve available for business expansion, paid personnel, outside services, independent assessment, marketing expenditure, or other discretionary commitments;

**WHEREAS**, the Company wishes to continue responsible market discovery and pursue lawful revenue and capital pathways without creating unfunded liabilities, misleading product claims, employment-law exposure, unauthorized securities activity, or obligations that the Company cannot perform;

**NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED**, subject to the Company's certificate of formation, company agreement/operating agreement, applicable law, and any required member or manager approvals, that:

1. **Zero-reserve baseline.** The Company acknowledges a zero-reserve planning posture. No expenditure, borrowing, guarantee, paid engagement, compensation promise, reimbursement promise, subscription, purchase, or other financial commitment is authorized by this resolution.

2. **No implied spending authority.** No founder, maintainer, project steward, contributor, employee, contractor, advisor, or other person may bind the Company to a financial obligation unless that obligation is covered by cleared, unrestricted Company funds and separately approved in a written transaction-specific authorization by the person(s) legally empowered to approve it.

3. **No-cost market discovery.** The Company may conduct non-binding customer interviews, demonstrations, product discovery, public technical education, waitlist collection, and requests for non-binding quotes or proposals, provided that:
   - no spend, payment obligation, exclusivity, or purchase commitment is created;
   - no investment or securities offering is solicited;
   - all statements about current functionality, security, scope, support, and certification are accurate;
   - SystemFabric 0.5.1 is described as **NOT_PRODUCTION_CERTIFIED / developer preview**; and
   - no customer is led to believe unfinished, mocked, disabled, or unvalidated functionality is generally available.

4. **Revenue-first commercial activity.** The Company may prepare and negotiate proposals for clearly scoped software engineering, architecture, integration, modernization, prototyping, testing, and related professional services. A binding statement of work, subscription, license, pilot, or customer prepayment may be accepted only after a separate written authorization confirms:
   - the authorized signatory and customer counterparty;
   - precise deliverables, exclusions, milestones, acceptance criteria, schedule, and change control;
   - the cost to perform, including tools, subcontractors, hosting, tax, insurance, and support;
   - the source and amount of funding sufficient to cover the next delivery stage's direct costs, applicable taxes/fees, and a reasonable refund/chargeback contingency. If a customer advance or milestone payment is intended to fund that stage, the agreement must make commencement and work authorization conditional on the funds actually clearing into a Company-controlled account; the receipt must be booked according to its legal/accounting character and kept available for the customer's delivery, refund, and other contractual obligations rather than treated as unrestricted cash;
   - data protection, security, confidentiality, IP, warranty, liability, refund, and termination terms; and
   - that the engagement does not depend on an unsupported production-readiness or certification claim.

5. **No unfunded recruitment.** The Company may invite expressions of interest and maintain a non-binding talent interest list. It may not advertise a funded position, make an employment offer, promise a start date, assign productive work, or represent that compensation is available unless a funded role and compliant written terms have first been approved. There is no authorized unpaid staffing, unpaid internship, or unpaid contractor program under this resolution.

6. **Worker classification and compensation.** Any future worker engagement must be reviewed on the actual facts under applicable wage-hour, tax, benefits, worker-classification, and state law. A contract label does not determine whether someone is an employee or independent contractor. Compensation must be specified in a signed agreement and funded before the work is authorized. Deferred compensation, commissions, revenue shares, bonuses, membership interests, profits interests, options, tokens, or other non-cash consideration are not authorized by this resolution.

7. **No informal equity or investment offers.** No person may offer, promise, sell, grant, or advertise membership interests, profits interests, notes, revenue participation, profit participation, or other investment rights on behalf of the Company under this resolution. Any capital raise or equity-linked talent arrangement requires a separate governing-authority approval and prior review by qualified securities, tax, and company counsel, including federal and applicable state securities-law requirements.

8. **No public donation program.** The Company does not establish a charitable donation channel or represent payments as tax-deductible donations. Sponsorship, grants, customer revenue, member contributions, member loans, and investment proceeds must each be documented and accounted for under their actual legal and tax character. No funds are to be sent to personal accounts or unofficial payment links.

9. **Vendor and assessor sourcing.** The Company may seek non-binding scope descriptions and fee estimates from independent security assessors, suppliers, and service providers, without authorizing work or incurring a fee. Any engagement must have a separate approved budget, written statement of work, conflict/independence review, confidentiality and data-handling terms, clear deliverables, and termination provisions. A sponsor or customer funding an assessment may not control its findings, severity ratings, conclusions, or retest independence.

10. **Funding acceptance controls.** The Company may not accept a grant, sponsorship, customer advance, contribution, loan, investment, or other funds until the recipient, account, legal basis, restrictions, deliverables, repayment/refund obligations, fees, tax/accounting treatment, and authorized administrator have been recorded and approved. Funds must be received in a Company-controlled account, not a personal account, and entered in the Company books.

11. **Treasury priority.** Once funds are received, the Company will first identify and reserve amounts needed for legally due taxes and filings, refunds/chargebacks, customer delivery, restricted grant/sponsorship purposes, payroll/contractor obligations, and other committed liabilities. Discretionary growth and new commitments may be considered only after these obligations are funded. A reserve target must be set from an actual cash forecast and cost baseline; no arbitrary percentage allocation is adopted here.

12. **Independent security evidence.** The first proposed third-party security-assurance expenditure, when funds permit, is the independent tenant/site isolation and authorization review tracked in GitHub issue #14. This resolution neither hires an assessor nor closes that gate. Assessment must use an authorized isolated environment, synthetic data, exact reviewed commit, written rules of engagement, independent reporting, and evidence acceptance under the release plan.

13. **Conflicts and disclosure.** Material conflicts involving a sponsor, customer, investor, supplier, assessor, or decision-maker must be disclosed and managed. No funding source receives automatic project ownership, maintainer status, security-report control, roadmap control, preferential access, or endorsement.

14. **No production-release waiver.** Nothing in this resolution approves a production release, changes SystemFabric's release scope, waives certification evidence, or changes the status **NOT_PRODUCTION_CERTIFIED / NO-GO**.

15. **Further approval.** Any expenditure, compensation plan, capital raise, borrowing, security issuance, material customer contract, or other obligation must be presented in a separate written approval that identifies the amount, funding source, authority, risk, terms, and accounting treatment.

16. **Records.** If adopted, the executed resolution and any subsequent transaction-specific approvals will be retained with the Company's governance records. A sanitized public policy summary may be published, but private member information, bank information, customer information, signatures, and confidential transaction terms must not be published.

**FURTHER RESOLVED**, that this resolution becomes effective only when approved and executed in the manner required by the Company's actual governing documents and applicable law. A GitHub pull request, issue comment, public roadmap, or maintainer action alone is not Company adoption.

## 4. Recommended funding sequence

The Company should pursue these paths in order, rather than begin with a public equity campaign or hire ahead of cash.

### A. Earn revenue from services firs

The lowest-friction near-term path is a founder-led services offer with bounded deliverables and little or no upfront infrastructure cost. Examples include:

- custom web application and API development;
- WordPress/PHP/JavaScript modernization and integration;
- architecture and technical-debt assessments;
- prototype builds and proof-of-concept integrations;
- test automation, migration planning, and reliability remediation.

This work can create actual customer value without requiring the Company to claim SystemFabric is production-certified. Separate client work from the SystemFabric preview and disclose the Company's ability and capacity honestly.

Prefer small fixed-scope discovery engagements or milestone contracts with an initial cleared payment that covers the next stage's direct costs. Do not use a customer's advance as unrestricted working capital where it is needed to deliver that customer's contracted work. Do not accept obligations that depend on hiring people or purchasing services the Company cannot yet fund.

### B. Secure paid design partners / preview evaluations

After market discovery identifies a real buyer and use case, consider one narrowly scoped, paid design-partner engagement. Keep the preview isolated and non-production; use synthetic data unless separately approved controls exist; limit the scope to demonstrable capabilities; define feedback rights and confidentiality; and avoid promising a production launch date or roadmap control.

A design-partner agreement is a commercial contract, not a donation and not an investment. It must describe exactly what the customer receives. Do not accept payment for unsupported functionality.

### C. Seek a restricted sponsorship or research partnership

A technology company, agency, university lab, customer, or other sponsor might underwrite a defined deliverable such as the independent security review, accessibility testing, or infrastructure. Use a written sponsorship/research agreement that identifies the legal recipient, scope, restrictions, fees, reporting and publicity rights. Keep assessor conclusions independent from the sponsor.

A sponsor should not receive ownership, control of findings, a security veto, exclusivity, or roadmap rights unless separately evaluated and approved. A restricted sponsorship is not unrestricted cash.

### D. Evaluate grants only against eligibility and timing

Some programs may permit for-profit LLC applicants; others require a nonprofit, university, research institution, specific ownership status, matching contribution, or cost-sharing. Verify current eligibility and reimbursement timing before applying. Do not treat a grant application, award announcement, reimbursement promise, or unpaid founder time as available cash. Do not incur costs before checking whether the award permits pre-award expenses.

### E. Member capital contribution or documented member loan

A member contribution or loan may be an option only if a member elects and can afford it, the Company agreement and approvals permit it, the terms and source are documented, and tax/accounting treatment is understood. Do not assume the owner should personally fund the Company or give a personal guarantee. The present resolution does not create a contribution obligation or loan.

### F. Outside investment only after preparation

Investment could be considered later, after the Company has a credible budget, product boundaries, IP ownership records, financial records, customer evidence and a use-of-proceeds plan. Membership interests, notes, SAFEs/convertible instruments, revenue participation and other investment arrangements can be securities or have complex LLC/tax consequences. Do not post investment terms publicly, promise returns, solicit investors, or circulate offering materials until qualified counsel selects and documents an available federal and state compliance pathway.

The SEC describes different exempt-offering routes with different solicitation, investor, intermediary, disclosure and filing requirements. For example, Rule 506(b) generally prohibits general solicitation; Rule 506(c) permits it only under conditions including accredited-investor status and reasonable verification; and Regulation Crowdfunding requires an SEC-registered broker-dealer or funding portal. These are not interchangeable shortcuts.

## 5. Talent and contributor strategy without cash

Do not confuse community building with staffing.

### Appropriate now

- Invite people to public discussions, technical feedback, issue triage, architecture conversations, and voluntary open-source contributions on the repository's published license and contribution terms.
- Publish a non-binding talent interest page describing roles the Company may seek **if funding is secured**.
- Identify prospective assessors and vendors, seek non-binding quotes, and maintain a transparent shortlist.
- Build a pipeline of potential paid candidates and B2B service providers without asking them to begin work.

### Not appropriate now

- Do not recruit people to perform routine productive work for Golden Obsidian LLC for free.
- Do not call a person a volunteer, advisor, intern, fellow, or contractor as a way to avoid minimum wage, overtime, payroll, tax, or classification duties.
- Do not imply that future employment, equity, revenue share, token allocations, or deferred compensation will be provided.
- Do not use a vague promise of future payment to get work started.

The U.S. Department of Labor says employees may not volunteer services to for-profit private-sector employers. Unpaid internship status is fact-specific and uses a flexible primary-beneficiary analysis; labels and disclaimers alone do not make an internship lawful. Worker classification is also fact-dependent. As of this draft date, the Department has published a 2026 proposed independent-contractor rule; a proposed rule is not itself a final rule. Recheck current federal and state requirements before any engagement.

A person independently choosing to contribute to a public open-source project is not a substitute staffing model. Keep contribution participation genuinely community-driven and governed by the existing contribution terms; do not assign unpaid commercial work or rely on contributors to fulfill customer obligations.

### When funding exists

Use a paid W-2 role where the actual relationship is employment. Use a genuine independent business only where the real working relationship supports that classification. Obtain payroll, tax, IP assignment/licensing, confidentiality, insurance, and employment advice as appropriate. The written agreement must match actual practice.

Equity or profits interests may be considered for a key long-term contributor only as a separately approved compensation and ownership plan. They are not cashless paperwork: they can dilute ownership, affect control, produce tax consequences, require company-agreement changes, and implicate securities rules.

## 6. Independent assessor funding plan

Issue #14 remains a required independent gate. Do not lower the review standard because the Company has no budget.

Recommended sequence:

1. **Now, at $0:** keep the already prepared assessor brief and vendor shortlist current; request only non-binding budgetary estimates and written scope assumptions. No chargeable consulting, pre-assessment, scanning, or testing is authorized.
2. **Build the real budget:** request comparable quotes from at least two qualified firms for the same scope: route/capability inventory, multi-tenant BOLA/IDOR testing, PostgreSQL/object storage, jobs/outbox, graph/cache, SSE/WebSocket, findings report, and Critical/High retesting. Ask for phases and a fixed-price baseline plus optional extensions.
3. **Seek an identified funding source:** preferably earned service revenue earmarked after delivery obligations are funded, a written sponsor underwriting the assessment, a qualifying research grant, or an approved voluntary member contribution. Do not assume a grant or sponsor will materialize.
4. **Approve a separate engagement:** record the selected firm, individual assessor, conflict check, cost ceiling, source of funds, statement of work, rules of engagement, data-handling terms, independence of findings, and retest terms.
5. **Engage only after cleared funds:** do not ask the assessor to begin on a promise of future payment.
6. **Accept evidence independently:** the assessor's findings and evidence must satisfy issue #14 and the formal release evidence contract. A quote, proposal, volunteer discussion, or vendor engagement by itself does not pass the gate.

A customer or sponsor may fund the assessment, but payment must not be conditioned on suppressing, downgrading, delaying, or pre-approving findings. The assessor must report to the Company under agreed independence protections.

## 7. Treasury and spending controls once money arrives

No percentage allocation or budget is adopted in this draft because no cash forecast, vendor quotes, tax position, recurring cost baseline, or delivery backlog has been supplied.

Before discretionary use, establish:

1. **A Company-controlled account** in the exact legal entity name, with authorized signers and basic fraud controls. Do not route business funds through a personal account.
2. **Bookkeeping from the first dollar.** Classify receipts as customer revenue, customer advances/deferred revenue, sponsorship, grant, member contribution, member loan, investment proceeds, or another substantiated category. Do not collapse all inflows into "donations."
3. **A 13-week cash forecast** updated weekly, showing cleared cash, expected receipts (discounted for uncertainty), tax and filing obligations, payroll/vendor commitments, refund/chargeback exposure, restricted funds and customer delivery costs.
4. **A liability-first reserve.** First fund tax and statutory obligations, committed payroll/vendor costs, delivery of prepaid customer work, refunds/chargebacks, restricted funding and debt service. Then establish an operating reserve target based on actual essential monthly costs and business risk.
5. **Written purchase approvals.** Each proposed expense must name the business purpose, vendor, total cost including renewal/tax/fees, funding source, budget line, cancellation terms, data access, owner, and approver.
6. **Segregation of duties where feasible.** The person who requests a purchase should not be the only person who approves and reconciles it. Where the Company is too small to separate duties, keep receipts, written approvals and monthly reconciliation.
7. **No recurring contracts by default.** Any subscription, hosting, SaaS, advertising, insurance, professional service, or vendor contract must disclose renewal date, cancellation window, total annualized cost, and termination consequences.
8. **Restricted funds remain restricted.** Do not divert grant, sponsor or customer-designated money to another purpose without written permission and appropriate accounting.
9. **No distributions during a cash squeeze.** Member distributions, reimbursements, or founder draws should not precede taxes, liabilities, customer obligations and the approved reserve; distributions also require review under the Company agreement and applicable law.
10. **Monthly governance report.** Record beginning/ending cash, inflows by category, committed obligations, aged receivables, restricted balances, runway, reserve status and any exceptions. Publish only a sanitized summary when useful and lawful.
